Some mornings you stand at the closet longer than the morning deserves. Nothing is wrong. You own plenty. You have somewhere to be that asks almost nothing of you. And still you stand there, pulling one thing out, holding it, putting it back.

![A slow morning, before the day has asked anything of you](/newsletter-images/barbie.gif)

It is the smallest decision in your day. What you wear to the desk, the grocery run, the coffee you have had a hundred times changes almost nothing. And somehow it is the one that wears you out before the day has even started.

---

## Why a small thing costs so much

Most decisions come with rails. The menu has a top and a bottom. The meeting has an agenda. The road has a lane. The closet has none of that. It opens the whole question at once, every piece against every other piece, no edges, no obvious place to begin.

> The closet doesn't ask you a question. It asks you all of them.

Then there is the timing. You make this one first, in the morning, before the coffee, when you are least equipped to make any decision at all. **A small problem handed to you at the worst possible hour,** every day, for the rest of your life.

---

## The usual fixes miss

So people try to fix it. Own less, and the closet gets smaller. Want less, and the wanting gets quieter. Care less, and the whole thing is supposed to stop mattering.

Each one goes after the closet. **None of them touch the decision,** which is the part that actually costs you. A smaller closet still asks the same question every morning. _It just asks it with fewer words._

---

## Set it down somewhere

This is the problem we started Oro to work on. Not what to buy, and not a tidier closet, but the eight in the morning version of it: what to do with the clothes you already have.

![The whole closet, running in your head at once](/newsletter-images/inside-out.gif)

The weight was never really the clothes. It is that you carry the whole closet in your head, every piece and every pairing, and you pick it up first thing in the morning, when you can least afford to hold anything.

> You don't need fewer clothes. You need to stop carrying all of them at once.

The fix is not to own less or want less. It is to put the question down. To let something else hold the closet, do the sorting, and hand you a place to begin, so you walk up to a start instead of a blank. **That is the part we want to take off you.**

---

The goal was never a smaller closet. It was a smaller decision.

You just want to be handed somewhere to start.

_- Oro_
