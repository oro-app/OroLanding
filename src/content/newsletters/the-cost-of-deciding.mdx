Some mornings you stand at the closet longer than the morning deserves. _Nothing is wrong._ You own plenty. You have somewhere to be that asks almost nothing of you. And still you stand there, **pulling one thing out, holding it, putting it back.**

![A slow morning, before the day has asked anything of you](/newsletter-images/barbie.gif)

It is **the smallest decision in your day.** What you wear to the desk, the grocery run, the coffee you have had a hundred times changes almost nothing. And somehow it is _the one that wears you out before the day has even started._

---

## Why a small thing costs so much

Most decisions come with rails. The menu has a top and a bottom. The meeting has an agenda. The road has a lane. **The closet has none of that.** It opens the whole question at once, _every piece against every other piece,_ no edges, no obvious place to begin.

> The closet doesn't ask you a question. It asks you all of them.

Then there is the timing. You make this one first, in the morning, before the coffee, _when you are least equipped to make any decision at all._ **A small problem handed to you at the worst possible hour,** every day, for the rest of your life.

---

## The usual fixes miss

So people try to fix it. _Own less,_ and the closet gets smaller. _Want less,_ and the wanting gets quieter. _Care less,_ and the whole thing is supposed to stop mattering.

Each one goes after the closet. **None of them touch the decision,** which is the part that actually costs you. A smaller closet still asks the same question every morning. _It just asks it with fewer words._

---

## Set it down somewhere

This is **the problem we started Oro to work on.** Not what to buy, and not a tidier closet, but the eight in the morning version of it: _what to do with the clothes you already have._

![The whole closet, running in your head at once](/newsletter-images/inside-out.gif)

**The weight was never really the clothes.** It is that you carry the whole closet in your head, every piece and every pairing, and you pick it up first thing in the morning, _when you can least afford to hold anything._

> You don't need fewer clothes. You need to stop juggling all of them at once.

The fix is not to own less or want less. It is to _put the question down._ To let something else hold the closet, do the sorting, and hand you a place to begin, so you walk up to a start instead of a blank. **That is the part we want to take off you.**

_- Oro_
